# Mechanical design overview

The thesis investigates a newborn jaw and neck mechanism with adjustable jaw opening, sliding, and resistance. A second aim is to study how changes in neck position affect jaw movement during a simulated intubation task. Representing newborn variation, including preterm and full-term infants, is a design target. Mechanical settings and ranges still need dedicated measurements and validation.

A candidate jaw mechanism uses a sliding carriage that carries a bearing-supported rotation shaft. A linear actuator or belt transmission can apply resistance to sliding, and a second actuator can apply torque at the shaft. Position and angle sensing can record the two coordinates. A fixed guide alone would prescribe the direction of translation, so adjustable guide arrangements and alternatives with independent degrees of freedom remain under consideration.

The video landmark analysis records how visible parts of a manikin move during one demonstration. It can inform candidate motion directions and highlight parts worth instrumenting in a future rig. Estimating physical displacement would require geometric calibration and a suitable reference frame. Evaluating resistance would require force or torque measurements under controlled conditions.
